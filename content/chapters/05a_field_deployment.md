# Deploying the Trilobit at Sea

This chapter explains how the Trilobit is brought to the seafloor from a vessel.

```{admonition} Heavy Equipment
:class: danger
A Trilobit weighs **54 kg** in air. Always lift and carry it with **at least two people**, both on land and on deck.
```

```{admonition} What You Need
:class: tip
**Hardware (per Trilobit)**

- A-frame (lifting frame for the Trilobit) with hooks
- 3 m rope between the A-frame and the weight
- Weight: 2 chains of 6 kg each
- Main rope: 1.5 × water depth
- Buoy
- Crane or, better, a winch (the same winch as for CTD casts works well)
```

## Before Going to Sea

Ideally, start the recording of the Trilobits on land before the cruise, as described in {ref}`04_start_recording`. Depending on the size of the vessel, there may be no good place on board to connect the Trilobits to the laptop and the control unit. It is perfectly fine to deploy them on the next day or even two days later. Just make sure to write down the start time of the recording.

Look at the **bathymetry** of the deployment area before the cruise and write down the water depth at each planned position, because it determines the rope length. Prepare the ropes in set segments; this makes it easier to shorten them during the deployment. For example, when you deploy in a shallow bay of around 30 m water depth, take 60 m segments and simply count the metres you have to cut off.

## Setting Up the Mooring

The Trilobit is deployed on a simple mooring. From the bottom to the top it looks like this:

1. **A-frame on the Trilobit.** Attach the A-frame to the Trilobit with its hooks at the **three outer bolts** of the housing. The A-frame is not solid but made of steel wires, so on the seafloor it collapses and rests on top of the Trilobit.
2. **3 m rope to the weight.** Tie a 3 m rope from the top of the A-frame to the weight (2 chains of 6 kg each).
3. **Main rope to the buoy.** From the weight, run the main rope up to the buoy. Its length should be **1.5 × the water depth**.

```{admonition} Example: 30 m Water Depth
:class: note
3 m (A-frame to weight) + 30 m (water depth) + 15 m (50 % extra) = **48 m of rope in total**.
```

```{admonition} Why the Weight?
:class: tip
The weight decouples the Trilobit from the rest of the mooring:

- The weight holds the A-frame down on the Trilobit and keeps the movements of the main rope away from it, so the Trilobit does not record the **noise** of the mooring all the time.
- When wind or currents pull on the buoy, the buoy drags the **weight** and not the Trilobit, which stays in place on the seafloor.
```

## Lowering the Trilobit to the Seafloor

1. Lift the Trilobit with at least two people to the deployment position on deck.
2. Hook the A-frame into the crane or winch.
3. Lower the Trilobit slowly and steadily into the water and down to the seafloor.
4. Attach the buoy to the top of the main rope and release it.
5. Write down the **deployment time** and the **position** (GPS coordinates). You will need them for the recovery and for the data processing.

## Picking Up the Trilobit

1. Find the buoy at the noted position and pick it up.
2. Haul the Trilobit up with the winch or crane, together with the weight and the A-frame. Use at least two people to bring it on deck.
3. Write down the **pick-up time**.
4. Clean the Trilobit thoroughly and remove **all the mud** from the housing and the inside. This takes some time, but it is necessary.

```{admonition} Do Not Disconnect the Batteries!
:class: danger
**Never disconnect the batteries right after the pick-up!** The Trilobit has to keep running until the recording has been stopped and the time has been corrected, see {ref}`05_recovery`. Only disconnect the batteries after that.
```
